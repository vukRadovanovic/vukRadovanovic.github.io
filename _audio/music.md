My musical journey began in the computer lab at my elementary school, where I spent ages playing around with Garageband on the big boxy iMacs we had in the computer lab.  I came home wanting to do more, but since we were exclusively a Windows family, I had to find some alternative.  After a bit of quick web searching, I found Acid Xpress 7.0, a freeware loop-based production software that suited my needs (and lack of skillset) perfectly. And as an added bonus, its website sported a wonderful frutiger aero aesthetic that the internet is particularly fond of these days.

![Internet Archive screenshot of the Acid Xpress download page](/assets/images/acid-xpress.png)

I wish I still had those old projects available, but I don't think I really understood the concept of saving my work yet - I would just sit there enjoying the loops I had made for a bit before closing the software, never actually exporting anything.  And even if I did save any .mp3s anywhere, those files unfortunately long lost.

I can't remember if it was my birthday, or Christmas, or some other occasion, but when I was 11 my dad gave me the best gift ever, the one that's kept on giving for years on end - a copy of FL Studio 11 with lifetime upgrades, that I'm still using to this day. (Hvala tata, voli te sin
